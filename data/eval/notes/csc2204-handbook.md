# CSC2204 Data Structures and Algorithms: Course Handbook

Semester 1. Course coordinator: Dr Mira Halvorsen. Teaching assistants: Joel Tan (tutorial groups T01 to T04) and Priya Raman (tutorial groups T05 to T08). Course forum: the CSC2204 channel on the department's discussion board. Email for admin questions: csc2204-staff@example.edu.

## About the course

CSC2204 covers the design and analysis of data structures and the algorithms that use them: asymptotic analysis, sorting, linked lists, stacks and queues, hash tables, binary search trees and their balanced variants, heaps and priority queues, union-find, graph traversal, shortest paths and minimum spanning trees. Programming is in Java 21. The course is worth 4 units and expects about 10 hours of work a week.

## Prerequisites

You need CSC1101 Programming Fundamentals and MAT1150 Discrete Mathematics, or their equivalents. MAT1150 matters most for the proofs and recurrence relations in weeks 2 and 3; if you took discrete maths elsewhere, check that it covered induction and big-O notation. Students without the prerequisites need the coordinator's written approval before the end of week 1.

## Final exam

The final exam is on 28 November at 09:00 in the multipurpose hall MPSH 2A. It lasts two hours, covers the whole course, and is closed book with two double-sided A4 sheets of notes allowed. Past papers from the last three years are on the course page. You must score at least 35% on the final exam to pass the course, whatever your other marks.

## Late submissions and extensions

Late problem sets and lab assignments lose 10% of the marks available for each day or part of a day they are late, up to 3 days. Work more than 3 days late receives zero. Extensions are only given for medical or compassionate reasons: email the staff address with a medical certificate or other evidence before the deadline where possible, and no later than 48 hours after it.

## Academic integrity

You may discuss problem sets with classmates at the level of ideas, but every line of code and every written answer you submit must be your own. Write the names of anyone you discussed a problem with at the top of your submission. Submissions are checked with a code similarity tool. Using AI tools to generate submitted code or answers is not allowed and is treated as plagiarism. A first offence means zero for the assignment and a report to the department; a second means failing the course.

## Textbook and resources

The main textbook is Introduction to Algorithms (Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest and Stein), 4th edition, ISBN 978-0262046305. It is optional: the lecture notes are self-contained, but the textbook chapters listed on the course page give more depth. VisuAlgo is recommended for visualising data structures. The course uses the department's online judge for lab assignments; you get 20 submissions per lab before penalties apply.
