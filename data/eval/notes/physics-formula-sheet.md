# Physics Formula Sheet

My formula sheet for the first-year physics exam, with what each symbol means and its SI unit.

## Mechanics

Newton's second law: F = ma. F is the resultant force in newtons (N), m the mass in kilograms (kg) and a the acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²). Newton's first law says an object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it; the third says that if A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.

The equations of motion (suvat) hold only for constant acceleration. s is displacement (m), u the initial velocity (m/s), v the final velocity (m/s), a the acceleration (m/s²) and t the time (s):

- v = u + at
- s = ut + ½at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- s = ½(u + v)t

Momentum: p = mv, in kilogram metres per second (kg m/s). Momentum is conserved in every collision when no external force acts; kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions. Impulse is FΔt, the change in momentum.

Work done: W = Fd cos θ, in joules (J), where d is the distance moved and θ the angle between the force and the direction of motion. Kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv². Gravitational potential energy: Ep = mgh, with g = 9.81 m/s² near the Earth's surface. Power: P = W/t, in watts (W).

## Springs and materials

Hooke's law: F = −kx. F is the restoring force (N), x the extension or compression from the natural length (m), and k the spring constant in newtons per metre (N/m), a measure of the spring's stiffness. The minus sign shows that the force acts opposite to the displacement. The law holds only up to the limit of proportionality. The elastic potential energy stored in a stretched spring is E = ½kx².

Young's modulus: E = stress / strain = (F/A) / (ΔL/L), in pascals (Pa). Stress is force per unit cross-sectional area; strain is extension divided by original length, and has no unit.

## Fluids

Pressure: p = F/A, in pascals (Pa), where 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Pressure in a liquid at depth h: p = ρgh, where ρ (rho) is the density in kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m³).

Bernoulli's equation for a steady flow of an incompressible fluid along a streamline: p + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. p is the pressure (Pa), ρ the density (kg/m³), v the flow speed (m/s) and h the height (m). It explains why pressure falls where a fluid speeds up, as over the curved top of an aerofoil or through the narrow throat of a Venturi meter.

Continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂. The volume flow rate is the same everywhere along a pipe, so a fluid speeds up where the pipe narrows.

## Waves and optics

Wave equation: v = fλ. v is the wave speed (m/s), f the frequency in hertz (Hz) and λ (lambda) the wavelength (m). Period: T = 1/f, in seconds.

Snell's law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ (written n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2). n₁ is the refractive index of the first medium, the one the light comes from, and n₂ the refractive index of the second medium, the one it enters. θ₁ is the angle of incidence and θ₂ the angle of refraction, both measured from the normal. Refractive index: n = c/v, where c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s is the speed of light in a vacuum; it has no unit.

Critical angle: sin C = n₂/n₁, for light going from a denser medium into a less dense one. Above the critical angle, total internal reflection happens, which is how optical fibres carry light.

## Electricity

Ohm's law: V = IR. V is the potential difference in volts (V), I the current in amperes (A) and R the resistance in ohms (Ω). It holds for an ohmic conductor at constant temperature.

Electrical power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R, in watts (W). Charge: Q = It, in coulombs (C).

Resistors in series: R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃. Resistors in parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃.

Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂/r². F is the electrostatic force between two point charges (N), q₁ and q₂ the charges (C), r the distance between them (m), and k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C² is Coulomb's constant, equal to 1/(4πε₀). Like charges repel and unlike charges attract.

## Thermal physics

Ideal gas law: PV = nRT. P is the pressure (Pa), V the volume (m³), n the amount of gas in moles (mol), T the absolute temperature in kelvin (K), and R = 8.314 J/(mol K) is the molar gas constant. Kelvin = Celsius + 273.15.

Specific heat capacity: Q = mcΔT. Q is the energy supplied (J), m the mass (kg), c the specific heat capacity in J/(kg K), and ΔT the temperature change (K). Water's specific heat capacity is about 4,200 J/(kg K).

Specific latent heat: Q = mL, the energy needed to change the state of a mass m without changing its temperature.
